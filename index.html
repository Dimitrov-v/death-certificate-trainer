The logic of Part I

  • Line a is the immediate cause — the final condition that resulted in death. Each line below answers "…due to (or as a consequence of) what?" The lowest completed line is the underlying cause of death — the disease that initiated the chain. One condition per line; read from bottom to top, the sequence must make etiologic and chronologic sense.
  • Never certify a mechanism as the cause. "Cardiac arrest," "cardiopulmonary arrest," "asystole," "PEA," and "respiratory arrest" describe how every patient dies, not why. NYC's system will reject or flag them, and they carry no information.
  • Terminal events and treatments are not causes. Intubation, pressors, CPR, comfort-care measures, and "multi-organ failure" do not belong in Part I.
  • No abbreviations. Write "pulmonary embolism," not "PE"; "congestive heart failure," not "CHF."
  • Be etiologically specific when the record supports it: organism for infections, site for cancers and hemorrhage, type for strokes (ischemic vs. hemorrhagic), etiology for cirrhosis and cardiomyopathy.
  • Intervals: give your best approximation ("minutes," "hours," "5 days," "years"). "Unknown" is acceptable — a blank is not. Intervals should generally lengthen as you move down Part I.
  • Vague conditions can't stand alone as the underlying cause: "renal failure," "respiratory failure," "hypotension," "sepsis" without a source, "old age," "failure to thrive." Ask yourself why the patient developed it and add that line.

Part II — Other significant conditions

  • Diseases that contributed to death but are not in the causal chain of Part I — e.g., COPD, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, dementia, malignancy not causing the death. Don't duplicate Part I entries here.

Manner of death & the NYC OCME

  • A hospital physician in NYC may certify natural deaths only. Any death that is not 100% the result of natural disease must be reported to the Office of Chief Medical Examiner (212-447-2030, 24/7) before a certificate is filed.
  • Reportable: criminal violence; any accident or injury — including falls — no matter how remote (NYC has no time limit: a hip fracture from a fall months ago still makes the death reportable); suicide; drug or chemical overdose/poisoning; sudden death in apparent good health; deaths in custody; deaths related to employment; deaths from diagnostic or therapeutic procedures/complications; suspicious or unusual circumstances; and deaths during or within one year of pregnancy.
  • The "but-for" test: but for the injury/event, would the patient have died when they did? If not, call the OCME. The OCME may decline the case and release it back to you — document the case number and the name of the person you spoke with.

Other eVital items

  • Did tobacco use contribute to death? Yes / No / Probably / Unknown — answer "Probably" or "Yes" for smoking-related deaths (COPD, lung cancer, many cardiovascular deaths).
  • For female decedents aged 10–54, a pregnancy status question must be answered; any death during or within one year of pregnancy is OCME-reportable.
  • eVital may show soft edit warnings highlighted in yellow; the certificate can still be filed, but fix real errors rather than overriding them.
